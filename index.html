Famous Solved Cases & Forensics

  • The Lindbergh Kidnapping (1932)

    The Crime: Charles Lindbergh Jr., the 20-month-old son of the famous aviator, was kidnapped from his home in New Jersey.

    How It Was Solved: Investigators tracked ransom money marked with specific gold certificates to a Bronx gas station, leading them to Richard Hauptmann. Forensic wood analysis matched the homemade wooden ladder left at the crime scene directly to floorboards in Hauptmann's attic.

  • The Golden State Killer / Joseph James DeAngelo (1970s to 1980s, Solved 2018)

    The Crime: A series of 13 murders, over 50 sexual assaults, and dozens of burglaries across California went unsolved for decades.

    How It Was Solved: In 2018, investigators used Investigative Genetic Genealogy. They uploaded crime scene DNA to public genealogy databases (GEDmatch), built out family trees, and identified ex-police officer Joseph James DeAngelo.

  • The Ted Bundy Case (1970s, Solved 1978)

    The Crime: Serial killer Ted Bundy murdered numerous young women across multiple states.

    How It Was Solved: Bundy was caught during a routine traffic stop in Florida. The critical evidence that secured his conviction for the Chi Omega sorority murders came from forensic odontology (bite mark analysis).

  • The Dr. Sam Sheppard Case (1954)

    The Crime: Marilyn Sheppard was murdered in her home in Ohio. Her husband, Dr. Sam Sheppard, claimed an intruder killed her. (This case inspired the movie The Fugitive).

    How It Was Solved: Though initially wrongfully convicted, bloodstain pattern analysis conducted during re-investigations identified a third person's blood at the scene, leading to legal exoneration and pioneering the use of blood spatter science in court.

  • The WannaCry Ransomware Attack (2017)

    The Crime: A global ransomware attack targeted computers running Microsoft Windows across 150 countries. It encrypted victims' files and demanded Bitcoin ransom payments to restore access.

    Impact: It severely crippled critical infrastructure, including the UK's National Health Service (NHS), FedEx, and major telecommunications networks.

    How It Was Solved / Contained: Marcus Hutchins, a British cybersecurity researcher, discovered an unregistered domain name hidden inside the ransomware code. By registering that domain, he accidentally activated a built-in "kill switch" that stopped the virus from spreading further. Later investigations attributed the malware to North Korea's Lazarus Group.

  • The Colonial Pipeline Cyberattack (2021)

    The Crime: DarkSide, a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) criminal group, launched a targeted attack on Colonial Pipeline, the largest fuel pipeline system in the United States.

    Impact: The company proactively shut down the pipeline for several days, causing gas panics, price spikes, and widespread shortages across the US East Coast. Colonial paid a ransom of 75 Bitcoin (~$4.4 million).

    How It Was Solved: The FBI traced the cryptocurrency transactions through the Bitcoin blockchain. By obtaining the private key to one of the hackers' cryptocurrency wallets, federal law enforcement managed to recover $2.3 million of the paid ransom.

  • The Silk Road Dark Web Marketplace (2011-2013)

    The Crime: Silk Road was an underground online marketplace operating on the Tor network (Dark Web) where users bought and sold illegal drugs, weapons, and stolen data using Bitcoin.

    How It Was Solved: A multi-agency task force (FBI, IRS, DEA) conducted digital forensic tracking and traced early online forum posts promoting the site back to an old personal email address. This led to the arrest of founder Ross Ulbricht ("Dread Pirate Roberts") in a San Francisco public library while logged into the site's administration panel.

  • The Yahoo Data Breaches (2013-2014)

    The Crime: Cybercriminals breached Yahoo's internal systems in two massive separate attacks, compromising over 3 billion user accounts—making it the largest data breach in history.

    Impact: Names, email addresses, phone numbers, security questions, and hashed passwords were stolen across billions of user accounts worldwide.

    How It Was Solved: The U.S. Department of Justice investigated the incident using international digital forensics and indicted four individuals, including officers from Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) and hired cybercriminals who exploited stolen web cookies to access user accounts without passwords.
